# CaptainApply — Logo Concept Brief

## The reference we're steering AWAY from

**Corsair Gaming** (corsair.com, PC hardware, $3B+ revenue):
- Three angular black sails, pointy/aggressive geometry
- Monochrome black
- Brand literalness: their sails point right (= "forward into speed/competition")
- "Bold spirit of PC gaming enthusiasts"
- Recognizable to every PC builder worldwide

That ship is taken. We need a different boat.

## What we WANT to say with the mark

**Brief from Lars:** "A boat sailing forward into unknown waters."

Encoded values:
- **Forward motion** (not stationary, not docked)
- **Open horizon** (uncertainty = opportunity, not fear)
- **Single vessel** (you, the Captain, in charge)
- **Subtle nautical, not pirate pageant** (chart under)

## Three direction concepts

### Option A — "Single triangular sail + horizon dot"

A small monogram-style mark:
- A single triangular sail (not 3 like Corsair) tilted slightly forward as if catching wind
- Below the sail, a single horizon dot or a thin curved line representing the waterline
- Optional: a tiny ⚓ anchor inside the sail, or no anchor at all — the "C" of Captain doubles as the sail's curve
- Color: gradient navy → cyan (matches HostSalt palette, brand-portfolio consistency)

**Differences from Corsair:**
| Element | Corsair | Option A |
|---|---|---|
| Number of sails | 3 | 1 |
| Sails | Filled triangles, black | Single outlined triangle, navy |
| Geometry | Sharp/aggressive | Soft/slight forward lean |
| Color | Solid black | Navy + cyan gradient |
| Mood | Speed/competition | Calm confidence |
| Extra element | None | Horizon dot / waterline curve |

**Why it works:** "1" instead of "3" sails = visually distinct at any size. The forward lean creates motion without aggression. Single vessel = "you, the Captain."

### Option B — "Bow wave + ascending arrow"

Geometric mark, no explicit boat:
- A curved bow-wave shape (looks like a wave + a ship hull merged)
- Inside or overlapping: a single ascending arrow or upward chevron
- The wave points up-right (forward + upward = growth)
- Two-tone: cyan waterline + white/navy arrow

**Differences from Corsair:**
- No sails at all (Corsair = sails; we're hull/wave)
- Focus on motion not vessel
- More abstract, more modern SaaS-feeling
- The arrow says "trajectory" not "voyage"

**Why it works:** Doesn't compete with Corsair visually. Reads as modern, tech, motion. The "rising arrow" is a strong SaaS trope (Linear, Stripe, Vercel all use it) but with a nautical twist.

### Option C — "Single sail C + dotted wake"

Wordmark + mark combined:
- The "C" of Captain drawn as a stylized single sail (one curve = one sail)
- Below the C: 3 small dots fading in size (the boat's wake trailing behind)
- "apply" written in clean Inter Tight lowercase next to it
- Color: cyan C + navy text

**Differences from Corsair:**
- Single sail (not 3)
- C-shape (curved, not triangular) = immediately distinct
- Has "trailing wake" dots = forward motion implied (dots go BEHIND)
- Combined wordmark = better for nav bar / favicon scenarios
- No black, no aggression

**Why it works:** "C for Captain" + "C for sail" + trailing wake = the logo IS the journey. Reads great at 16px favicon and 1024px hero. Stays inside the brand-portfolio (HostSalt = navy + cyan, same palette).

---

## Recommendation

**Option C** for the full brand (wordmark + sail C + wake dots).

**Why:**
- Tells the story in one mark (boat, forward motion, single vessel)
- Stays 100% distinct from Corsair (1 curved C, not 3 angular sails)
- Fits a SaaS dashboard where the mark sits in the nav bar, the favicon, the loading screen, and the mobile home-screen icon
- The "C" being the sail = brilliant double-meaning for the brand
- Trailing dots = "we charted the course for you, your move next"

**Plus a separate navigation icon** (just the C+sail, no wake dots) for the nav bar and favicon.

## What the mark should NOT do

- ❌ Look like a bird (no wings)
- ❌ Look like a paper plane (too "telegram bot")
- ❌ Look like a paperclip (too "office tool")
- ❌ Look like a paper boat (too kid-toy)
- ❌ Look like a speedboat (too consumer-luxury)
- ❌ Look like a yacht (too elite)
- ❌ Look like the Stripe logo (too derivative)
- ❌ Look like the Linear logo (too derivative)
- ❌ Look like Corsair's 3-sail mark (collision)

## What the mark SHOULD do

- ✅ Say "forward" without saying "fast"
- ✅ Say "nautical" without saying "pirate"
- ✅ Say "one vessel" (you, the Captain)
- ✅ Work at 16px (favicon)
- ✅ Work in monochrome (printing, single-color icons)
- ✅ Work in reverse (light on dark for dark mode)
- ✅ Be drawn in 5 seconds (a child could sketch it)
